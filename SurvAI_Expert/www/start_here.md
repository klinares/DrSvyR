# SurvAI Expert

**A survey methodologist to consult whenever you produce a survey estimate.**

Every number that comes from a survey, such as a percentage, an average or a
total, differs from the truth for more than one reason. The Total Survey
Error framework is the standard way to name those reasons, so that each can
be checked instead of overlooked. This tool is built around it.

## The sources of error it helps you think through

| Source of error | The question to ask |
|---|---|
| Coverage | Could everyone in the population have been reached? |
| Sampling | How were people selected, and how much would the result change with a different sample? |
| Nonresponse | Who did not answer, and are they different from those who did? |
| Validity | Does the question measure what you mean to measure? |
| Measurement | Did people answer accurately, and did the interviewer or the mode affect them? |
| Processing | Were answers coded, edited and cleaned without changing them? |
| Adjustment | Do the weights correct the problems above, or add new ones? |

For data that was not collected by a survey, such as administrative records
or data from apps and websites, it asks the matching questions: who ends up
in the data, how records were linked, and how the system that produced the
data shaped it.

## What you can do here

- **Ask.** Put a question in plain words: how to design a sample, how large
  it should be, how to weight, how to estimate a result and its margin of
  error, or how to judge the quality of a data source. You get a short
  answer, the formula if there is one, and example code if you ask for it.
- **Formulas.** Look up a formula by name, see what each symbol means and
  when it applies.
- **Report reader.** Upload a survey's technical report and get a plain
  summary of how the sample was drawn, how the weights were built, and what
  you must do to analyze the data correctly.

## What it draws on

A library of teaching material on how surveys are designed, carried out
and analyzed: several thousand passages, formulas and worked examples,
each kept with the page it came from. The Methodology tab describes it.

## How it keeps answers accurate

- It answers from the library, and each statement shows the source it came
  from. Click a source to read the original passage; you can enlarge it.
- Formulas are copied from the library exactly. They are never retyped.
- If the library does not cover your question, it says so. Anything it adds
  from outside the library is labelled "Not from the library", and text with a
  dotted underline is not tied to a source.

## What it does not do

It does not see or run your data, and it does not replace a methodologist
for decisions that carry weight. Treat it as a well-read colleague: check
the sources it shows you.
