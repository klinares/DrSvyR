**November 13 2024, Survey satisficing**
* Satisficing, not optimal, satisfactory efforts, when cognitive effort is required.
  * Examples are strightlining, primacy, DK, mental coin flipping, acquiescence (reluctant acceptance(.
  * Two types
    * weak satisficing – superficial execution of response process, not carefully.
    * Strong satisficing – entire steps of the response process left out.
      * Most likely when task is difficult, factors ability of respondent and motivation.
* What is weak satisficing?
  * Primacy, and agreeing with interviewer assertion.
* What is strong satisficing?
  * Endorsing status quo, non-differentiation responses (straightening) ratings, DK, and randomly responding.

$$p(\text{satisficing}) = \frac{a_1(\text{Task Difficulty})}{a_2(\text{Ability}) \times a_3(\text{Motivation})}$$

* Task difficulty – response options, technical words in the questions, # of category, and retrieval efforts lead to difficulty.
* Respondent ability – cognitive sophistication, extant to someone’s knowledge on the topic, having preformed attitudes.
* Motivation: Relevance of survey topic. Incentive in general. Length and mode. Motivation varies across the survey.

<!-- page 11 -->

* Theory of satisficing: people rarely have access to all information, cannot process all relevant information.
  * Bounded rationality notion, we are not fully rational all the time, limited amounts of information to make decisions. We have to optimize everything, we would overload ourselves.
* Primacy – one situation were weak satisficing can occur. Response order often used in lower educated individuals.
  * R’s mind gets cluttered after reading and considering options, later options cannot be processed. End up with acceptable answers. The first item gets the deepest process, likely to be selected.
* Human computer interaction literature to understand level of effort people take during a task.
  * Minimal action/effort is often avoided at the expense of accuracy.
* Recall conrad et al 2006, fewer clicks to get definition of a technical word, people would use, when more clicks required, they did not request definitions.
* Satisficing vs satisficing indicators
  * some behaviors, DK response. Others are indirect such as primacy effect. Some behaviors are associated such as speeding and straightlining suggest shortcuts.
  * Satisficing can be situational, maybe when fatigue kicks in.

**Culture**

Culture matters to survey measurement through the role of cognition in survey response process

* Among Germans and Chinese, when asked about life academic and general satisfaction, Chinese were more attentive to the questionnaire context, making a pragmatic decisions, and translation cannot solve problem.
  * Collective culture more attune to the survey context to provide nonredundancy.
* Culture main influence all processes in the survey cognition process.
  * In collectivist, people are attune to fit in, which affects how they respond to private events. Individualist people tend to make more references to internal states and emotions, while collectivist provide more reference to context and event.
    * Lee & Schwarz, general health question, when questions about specific health reports comes before general health, Spanish speakers reduce their self rating than the opposite. This was not the case for English speakers. Difference was in the interpretation of the construct, maybe health was perceived more widely for Spanish speakers than English.
* Response
  * content considered favorable differs by culture, social desirability can vary and impact editing.

<!-- page 20 -->

* Individualist culture encourage view of self as unique and positive terms, display self-enhancement bias of self, value honesty in interaction with others.
  * May value assertiveness and independence which will be viewed as positive.
* Collectivist – more concern about harmony and saving face, editing of the truth is considered acceptable in the interest of maintaining honor, harmony, and saving face. More impression management.
* Time orientation, subjective life expectancy, western cultures more future oriented, non-western tend to have a past orientation. The Lee study, found that time orientation and how much they think about the future influences Rs willingness or ability to influence their response.
* Measurement error, different groups use response scales differently. Ex. Self report on pain in past 30 days. When there is heterogeneity, because of their background, might answer differently to the same question because of the different thresholds vary across groups. Sometimes the translation can trigger heterogeneity.
* Goals in survey measurement
  * $y_{it} = \mu_i + \epsilon_{it}$
    * $t$: Time element;
    * $y_{it}$: Measured value of subject $i$ at time $t$ ;
    * $\mu_i$: "true" value of subject $i$; and
    * $\epsilon_{it}$: measurement error at time $t$ for subject $i$
  * Measurement evaluation criteria for $y$ :
    * $\text{Validity}(y) = Cor(y_i, \mu_i)$
    * $\text{Reliability}(y) = \frac{Var(\mu_i)}{Var(y_i)}$
    * $\text{Bias}(y) = E_i[E_t(y_{i,t}) - \mu_i]$
  * Goal is to have comparable measurements, or error within our groups.
  * When we have functional equivalence, the scale measures the same construct in every group.
  * Measurement invariance, evaluate equivalence of a measured construct in different groups.
