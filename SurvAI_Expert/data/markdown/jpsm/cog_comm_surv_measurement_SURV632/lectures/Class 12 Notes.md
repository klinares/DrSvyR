# Adaptation and Translation

* Questions/questionnaire are developed in one particular survey context (e.g., one population, one mode, one language, one culture)
* Adaptation: deliberate modification of a question or questionnaire to reflect survey contexts
* Translation: From “source” to “target” language
* Example:
  * Translation: English to French
  * Adaptation: French-French vs. Canadian-French
  * “How proud are you to be [nationality]?”

# Adaptation: Rationales – 1

* For measurement units
  * E.g., Metric vs. imperial

  * E.g., Vegetables: green salad, bok choy, komatuna

# Adaptation: Rationales – 2

* To respect cultural discourse norms
  * E.g., Korean with a honorific system
* To accommodate cultural sensibilities
  * Culturally anchored norms (e.g., writing directions)
* To reflect lexicon and grammar
  * E.g., Neither agree or disagree
* To maintain/reduce difficulty

# Adaptation Example

* Agreement scale response categories frequently use “neither agree nor disagree”
* In languages such as Hebrew :
  * Closest semantic option available to translate ‘disagree’ in Hebrew, for example, corresponds to ‘no agree’
  * ‘neither’ and ‘nor’ are the same as the target language element corresponding to ‘no’
  * If translated into “no agree, no no agree: makes little sense in Hebrew
  * ‘neither agree nor disagree’ translated into ‘in the middle’ (Harkness, 2003)

# What is "Culture"?

# Characterizing Culture – 1

* Cultural dimensions (Hofestede)
  * A framework for cross-cultural communication
  * Describes effects of a society's culture on the values of its members, and how these values relate to behavior
* Cultural syndromes (Triandis)
  * Consist of shared attitudes, beliefs, norms, role and self definitions, and values of members of each culture that are organized around a theme
    * e.g., individualism/collectivism
    * e.g., emotional expression/suppression
* Cultural norms
  * Rules or expectations of behavior and thoughts based on shared beliefs within a specific cultural or social group
* Cultural values
  * Preferences people have for how they navigate life
  * Indicate how people prefer to interact, communicate, plan, and complete tasks.

# Characterizing Culture – 2

## 6 <u>orthogonal</u> cultural dimensions by Hofestede

1. Individualism vs. Collectivism
Degree to which people in a society are integrated into groups

2. Power distance: Small vs. Large
Extent to which the less powerful members of a society accept/expect that power is evenly distributed

3. Uncertainty avoidance: Weak vs. Strong
Society’s tolerance to ambiguity

4. Masculinity vs. Femininity
Men should & women may be assertive/ambitious vs. both sexes modest/caring

5. Long-term vs. Short-term orientation
Important life events will occur in the future vs. in the past or now

6. Indulgence vs. Restraint
High vs. low importance on leisure

# Goals in Survey Measurements – 1

* Goal: Error-free measurement
* Simple measurement error model
  $y_i = \mu_i + \epsilon_i$, where
  * $y_i$: measured value of subject $i$;
  * $\mu_i$: “true” value of subject $i$; and
  * $\epsilon_i$: measurement error - Distribution of $\epsilon_i$ determines the nature of measurement error
* Function of $y_i$ in the analysis

* E.g., Self-reported weight ($y_1$) and height ($y_2$)
  vs. BMI ($z = f(y_1, y_2)$)

<!-- page 45 -->

45

# Goals in Survey Measurements – 2

* $y_{it} = \mu_i + \epsilon_{it}$
  * $t$: Time element;
  * $y_{it}$: Measured value of subject $i$ at time $t$ ;
  * $\mu_i$: “true” value of subject $i$; and
  * $\epsilon_{it}$: measurement error at time $t$ for subject $i$
* Measurement evaluation criteria for $y$ :
  * $\text{Validity}(y) = Cor(y_i, \mu_i)$
  * $\text{Reliability}(y) = \frac{Var(\mu_i)}{Var(y_i)}$
  * $\text{Bias}(y) = E_i[E_t(y_{i,t}) - \mu_i]$

# Survey Measurements for 3MC – 1

* Goal: Comparable measurement/error
* When groups $A$ and $B$ are involved,
  $y_{Ai} = \mu_{Ai} + \epsilon_{Ai}$ and $y_{Bi} = \mu_{Bi} + \epsilon_{Bi}$
* Comparability on the errors, $\epsilon_{Ai}$ and $\epsilon_{Bi}$, determines measurement comparability
* Functional equivalence: the scale measures the same construct in every group
* Measurement invariance test can evaluate the equivalence of a measured construct in different groups across cultures

# Measurement Invariance

- Measurement invariance (MI) test: examine and ensure the comparability of multi-item scales across groups
- Multi-item scale: a set of differently worded question items related conceptually, developed to measure the same construct, and can be aggregated into a total score
- MI examines
  - Whether a scale measures the same underlying construct with the same structure across groups
  - Whether survey question items have the same meaning for male and female, elderly and teenagers, and people with different race/ethnicity and cultural backgrounds...

# Multiple-Group Confirmatory Factor Analysis (MGCFA)

* Continuous, ordinal or binary distributed indicators
* Four levels of MI:
  * Configural invariance (least restrictive)
  * Metric / weak invariance
  * Scalar / strong invariance
  * Strict invariance (most restrictive)
* The first three levels are essential for latent mean comparisons across groups

<!-- page 52 -->

52

# MGCFA - Configural invariance

Figure 1. An illustration of a single-factor CFA with $p$ indicators.

Latent Variable

$\lambda_1$ $\lambda_2$ $\dots$ $\lambda_p$

$Y_1$ $Y_2$ $\dots$ $Y_p$

$\varepsilon_1$ $\varepsilon_2$ $\dots$ $\varepsilon_p$

$$Y_g = \tau_g + \Lambda_g \eta + \varepsilon_g$$

* $Y$ is a $p \times 1$ vector of scores on the $p$ observed indicators
* $\tau$ is a $p \times 1$ vector of intercepts where $p$ is the number of indicators
* $\Lambda$ is a vector of factor loadings
* $\eta$ is the latent score
* $\varepsilon$ is a $p \times 1$ vector of residuals or error terms representing the unique portion of the observed variables not explained by the common latent variables
* $g$, group subscript, indicating that each of the parameters can differ by group

# MGCFA - Metric invariance

Figure 1. An illustration of a single-factor CFA with $p$ indicators.

$$Y_g = \tau_g + \Lambda\eta + \varepsilon_g$$

* Requires the factor loadings ($\Lambda$) to be equal across comparison groups
* Full or partial metric invariance

# MGCFA - Scalar invariance

Figure 1. An illustration of a single-factor CFA with $p$ indicators.

$$Y_g = \tau + \Lambda \eta + \varepsilon_g$$

* Requires not only the factor loadings but also the item intercepts ($\tau$) to be invariant across groups
* Final level of MI-testing that is required to be able to compare latent means across groups
* Only with scalar invariance (at least partial scalar invariance), one can more confidently conduct latent means comparisons across groups

<!-- page 55 -->

# MGCFA - Strict invariance

Figure 1. An illustration of a single-factor CFA with $p$ indicators.

$$Y_g = \tau + \Lambda\eta + \varepsilon$$

* Assess whether there is group invariance with respect to the unique residual variance ($\varepsilon$)
* Necessary in order to attribute group differences in the covariance structure (variances and covariances/correlations) of the observed indicators to the corresponding differences at the latent variable level

Melipillán, E. R., & Hu, M. (2020). Measurement Invariance Across Groups. SAGE Publications Limited. https://methods-sagepub-com-christuniversity.knimbus.com/foundations/measurement-invariance-across-groups

# Summary

* Observed cultural differences in survey responses may be
  * True differences in attitudes or behaviors
  * Differences in response process
  * Differences in different methods used (such as mode)
  * Mixture of all
  * Long been recognized as an issue, but methodological research is relatively recent and limited
* Investigate potential reporting heterogeneity and evaluate measurement invariance before comparing across cultural groups!
