# MSE in the TSE context

* Aggregating error
  - Provides information to data users regarding the overall quality of reported estimates
  - Example: $MSE(\bar{y}) = B_{NR}^2 + B_{Meas}^2 + Var_{Sam}$
    * Incomplete, but potentially useful

* Decomposing error
  - Useful for understanding and targeting major sources of error
  - Example: decomposing *selection* error into coverage error and nonresponse error
    * $B(\hat{\theta}) = B_{Cov} + B_{NR}$

# Studies of multiple sources of survey error: Key objectives

* Need to understand the relative magnitudes of each error source in various survey estimates; optimize resource allocation to improve estimates
* Error to think that error sources are always independent.
* Need to understand the interplay between errors as a function of survey design features; e.g., are additional call attempts beneficial ?
* Ideally, obtain a causal understanding of the relationship between survey errors

# Challenges

* Typically, no repetition
* Study of a source of error often requires a special design.
  * What do you need to estimate non-response bias?
  * What do you need to estimate measurement error?
  * What do you need to gauge the impact of two different questionnaire wordings?
* The possible association between sources of error requires designs that allow **simultaneous** estimation of the multiple sources of error under investigation.

# Conceptual models of correlation between NRE and ME

* Independence model: NR due to situational or motivational factors but ME largely due to cognitive factors. Thus, errors are thought to be generated independently.

* But is that the case ? Remember the satisficing formula. Hence the ‘common cause’ model.

# Sakshaug, Yan, & Tourangeau (2010)

* Somewhat reverse fashion -> do efforts at reducing measurement error increase non-response error ?
* Survey of UMD alumni (admin records available) using CATI, IVR, and a web survey.
* Socially undesirable items: Measurement bias ($8$ - $20$ percentage points) $>$ NR bias ($2$ – $3$ points)
* Socially desirable and neutral items: NR bias ($0$ – $15$ points) $>$ measurement bias ($0$ – $7$ points)
* Switching to self-administered modes can reduce ME but increase overall error due to drop-offs.
* Additional call-backs reduce noncontact bias but less consistent relationship with ME.

# Tradeoff/Interplay between Error Sources

* Design features may affect more than one source of error.
* TSE perspective: base design decisions on overall effects - not just their effects on one source of error.
* Since bias is directional, individual errors can:
  - accentuate each other, such that net bias $>$ sum of absolute biases
  - be independent
  - cancel each other out, such that net bias $\le$ sum of absolute biases
