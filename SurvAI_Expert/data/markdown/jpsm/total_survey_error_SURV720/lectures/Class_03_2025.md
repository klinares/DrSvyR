# But coverage rate != coverage bias

Amaya et al. (2021) assess ABS for RECS 2015

Step 1:. Assess coverage rate within each sampled segment by comparing fieldable residential addresses to the ACS HU count.

Step 2: Use one of three frames: ABS only (if est. cov. rate > 90%), enhanced listing (if 56% < ECR <= 90%), field listing (if ECR <= 56%).
- Observed coverage rates are 100%, 96%, 25%

# Amaya et al. (2021)...

Step 3: Build a population based on the RECS sample.

Step 4: Fit a logistic model: covered by original ABS (yes, no) ~ address and block group information from ACS

Step 5: Construct 100 frames with coverages ranging from 1 – 100% by dropping bottom x% of the pop. based on propensity.

# 1. Redefine the target population

Define the target population in a way that is compatible with the sample frame

* Redefine scope of the target population

  - Head and spinal cord injury victims -> head and spinal cord injury victims who required hospitalization
  - Credit card users -> credit card users banking with the University of Michigan Credit Union

* Redefine the population units
  - X users -> tweets
  - visitors (to a museum, business, web site) -> visits

# Half-Open Interval (HOI)

* Adds housing units to an address frame by searching for new or missed units in the interval between the selected unit and next unit in the frame
  – Why “Half” ?
  – Used for in-person interviewing; can be applied to either area sampling or ABS frames
  – Procedure is carried out by interviewers during data collection

* What do we mean by “hard-to-reach”?
* Multiplicity
  – Respondents report about events among those in their personal networks (i.e., acquaintances, friends, extended family)
* Snowball sampling (non-probability)
  – Initial recruits in convenience sample refer to other members in the population.

# Respondent-driven sampling (RDS)

* Effort to convert snowball sampling into a probability sampling method
* Recruit ‘seeds’ who then recruit others in their network

Source: Liu, Li, Ha, and Li (2012)

<!-- page 36 -->

# RDS…

* Must be possible to trace who recruited whom
* Should collect information concerning the size of each person’s network;
* Must have a maximum number of possible recruitments for a person
* Recruiter and recruit must have known each other prior to recruitment, each being a member of the other’s social network.
* But not fully straightforward…

authors reported their results. There are <u>122 variables for which the estimate for the internet population is at least double that for the non-internet population, or vice versa</u>. To name just a few: own a computer (82% of the internet population vs. 20% for the non-internet population); went to see a movie in the past year (75% vs. 32%); own stocks (71% vs. 28%), became a U.S. citizen before 1960 (4% vs. 36%); importance to choose products politically (not important = 5% vs. 25%); and has given to charity (77% vs. 42%). <u>Given these wide gaps, even with the non-internet population under 10 percent of the total population, a survey with only Internet respondents would experience bias of as much as 9% on these measures</u>, and 105 variables would exhibit a bias of at least 5 percent.
