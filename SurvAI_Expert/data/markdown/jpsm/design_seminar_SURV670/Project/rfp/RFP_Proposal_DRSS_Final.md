## B. Introduction

Dead Reckoning Survey Solutions, LLC (DRSS) is a minority-veteran-owned survey research firm and Equal Opportunity Employer. We are submitting this proposal in response to the State of Connecticut's Request for Proposals for Onboard Customer Satisfaction Surveys of the CTtransit fixed bus route system. DRSS specializes in marketing research for public transit agencies, including work for clients such as the Washington Metropolitan Area Transit Authority (WMATA), where we have designed and executed customer satisfaction studies and product performance evaluations.

CTtransit operates 183 fixed bus routes across ten service areas spanning Connecticut. CTDOT needs to understand who is riding, where they travel, and how they perceive their experience to inform decisions about service, investment, and equity. The survey must collect Title VI demographics, trip characteristics, and satisfaction data in six languages, through paper and digital modes, in an ADA-compliant format while protecting anonymity, all within three years and a \$2,000,000 budget.

Our proposal combines methodological rigor (a stratified probability sampling plan calibrated to p\<0.05), technological innovation (AI-powered OCR, real-time QA/QC, NLP coding, and AVL data integration), and cost efficiency through a phased rotation that concentrates field resources within annual waves. We also propose an optional smartphone-based rider experience panel.

The sections that follow describe our firm's qualifications, our team, the proposed methodology in detail, the division of responsibilities between DRSS and CTtransit, and suggested modifications to the scope of services. Budget exhibits, a detailed budget justification, hourly rate schedules, and a 36-month GANTT chart are provided in the appendices.

## E. Proposed Methodology

Within four months of the Effective Date, DRSS will deliver a Survey Management Plan to CTDOT including all RFP-required elements: survey schedule, administration plan (with pilot test and outreach), staff management plan, resource plan, QA/QC plan, and data security plan. Each component is described below.

### Sampling Design

The survey sampling plan is built around a stratified probability design that targets approximately 5,150 completed intercept surveys across all ten CTtransit service areas over the three-year project. The stratification structure ensures representative coverage by service area, day type (e.g., weekday and weekend), and time period (e.g., AM Peak, Midday, PM Peak, and Evening on weekdays; Daytime and Evening on weekends, as defined in the RFP). Areas with more routes and higher ridership receive larger samples: Hartford is targeted at approximately 1,000 completed surveys, New Haven at 700, and the mid-sized areas (e.g., Stamford, Waterbury, New Britain) in the range of 400--600 each. The smaller systems such as Bristol, Wallingford, and Meriden receive samples in the range of 250--350, sufficient for area-level inference at approximately ±5% margin of error with 95% confidence.

We propose a phased rotation in three annual waves: Year 1 covers Bristol, Wallingford, Meriden, and CTfastrak (19 routes, 1,350 surveys); Year 2 covers New Britain, Stamford, Waterbury, and Express Bus (83 routes, 2,100 surveys); Year 3 covers Hartford and New Haven (81 routes, 1,700 surveys) and concludes with the Final Systemwide Report. Starting with smaller areas lets the team refine operations before taking on the larger systems. Each wave is surveyed once per twelve-month cycle, and the Systemwide Report integrates all three years.

The sampling plan also addresses several sources of bias common in onboard intercept surveys. Time-of-day coverage prevents overrepresentation of peak commuters. Weekend sampling captures recreational, shopping, and other non-commute trip purposes that might otherwise be invisible. Targeted oversampling on routes serving low income neighborhoods and underrepresented communities ensures that the Title VI demographic data reflects the full diversity of CTtransit's ridership.

### Survey Instrument and Questionnaire Development

A single questionnaire will be used across all ten service areas, as confirmed by CTDOT. Mr. Linares will lead instrument development in collaboration with CTDOT, incorporating all required data elements: Title VI demographics (race, national origin, English proficiency, language spoken at home, household income), travel information (trip purpose, boarding location, alighting location, mode of access to transit, mode of egress, fare payment method), service experience ratings (on-time performance, transfer convenience, frequency, service coverage, bus stop and shelter cleanliness, service communications, and ease of fare payment), and onboard experience ratings (comfort, driver professionalism, bus cleanliness, and bus safety). The instrument will also incorporate an informed consent procedure, which CTDOT confirmed is required.

We estimate the average length of an interview at 8 to 10 minutes, which is about the most riders will sit through that can be reliably completed onboard during a typical transit trip without generating excessive item nonresponse or burden. Satisfaction items will use a five-point Likert scale ranging from Very Satisfied to Very Dissatisfied, with a neutral midpoint. DRSS will develop a coding scheme for all response choices and answers to facilitate data entry, digitization, and processing. All response options will be pre-coded with numeric values mapped to each answer category. We also recommend including one or two optional open-ended questions, such as asking riders what single improvement would most enhance their CTtransit experience, to capture qualitative feedback that structured items cannot fully represent. These responses will be coded using NLP-based thematic analysis, as described in the data processing section below.

The instrument will be prepared in English, Spanish, Polish, Brazilian Portuguese, Haitian Creole, and Simplified Chinese, with certified translations verified through cognitive pretesting. Surveyors will have access to online translation tools on their tablets. The approach to carrying physical paper copies versus using the digital platform as the default multilingual interface will be finalized in the Survey Management Plan.

The survey will be fully ADA-compliant, with large print versions available and surveyor assisted administration for passengers with visual or cognitive impairments. Regarding short trips where a rider cannot complete the full survey onboard, DRSS will provide a QR code card allowing completion within 60 minutes of boarding. The QR code generates a timed session, and if a rider is timed out, the system provides a mechanism for restarting. Surveys not completed within the allotted time are rejected in whole. CTDOT left the question of off-board completion as a design consideration for the contractor. Our position is that the QR code mechanism, combined with the time limit, strikes the right balance as it allows riders on short trips to finish after alighting without compromising the integrity of the onboard experience by permitting indefinite post trip completion. We estimate response rates of 55 to 65 percent for paper surveys and 20 to 30 percent for QR code surveys, based on published benchmarks from comparable transit intercept studies, and the sampling plan calibrates distribution attempts accordingly.

### Survey Administration

The pilot test runs from Month 5 through Month 6 on a subset of Year 1 routes, testing both survey modes, the sampling methodology, the AI validation engine, and a small smartphone panel deployment. The Pilot Test Assessment Report documents findings and recommended changes, subject to CTDOT approval.

Following pilot approval, full survey administration proceeds according to the finalized Survey Management Plan. For each service area in the year's wave, Ms. Hawthorne and Mr. Dixon deploy on-site supervisors who manage daily field operations, for instance assigning surveyors to correct routes and time periods, tracking completions against sampling targets in real time, collecting and reviewing paper surveys at the end of each shift, and troubleshooting any technology issues with tablets or QR codes. Surveyors board buses using the CTtransit passes that CTDOT confirmed will be provided, inform passengers about the survey, provide assistance in completing it, and ensure that paper surveys are completed onboard.

Each respondent receives a complimentary CTtransit All-Day Pass (\$3.50) as an anonymous incentive, budgeted by DRSS within the \$2,000,000 ceiling as confirmed at the Bidders Conference. The total incentive cost across three years is approximately \$18,025.

To prevent duplicate responses as required by the RFP, surveyors ask a verbal screening question and the QR code platform displays a similar prompt. Device fingerprinting on digital surveys flags potential duplicates for review. The smartphone experience panel, described below, provides a way to capture repeat rider encounters as longitudinal data rather than treating them as contamination.

DRSS submits weekly status reports documenting completions by route and time period, under-sampled routes, and corrective actions. Supervisors reassign staff daily to correct deficiencies, and a reserve pool of surveyors and tablets covers absences and equipment failures.

### Staff Management and Resource Plan

Ms. Hawthorne oversees all aspects of staff management for the project. This includes recruiting surveyors from the communities served by CTtransit, with particular attention to hiring multilingual candidates, such as conducting background screening, managing compensation and scheduling, and monitoring daily performance. In each service area, DRSS will hire locally to the extent possible and maintain a reserve pool of trained surveyors who can be deployed on short notice to cover absences or address under-sampled routes. Field supervisors report to Mr. Dixon and are responsible for the day-to-day direction of surveyors in each service area.

The resource plan covers all equipment, tools, and supplies necessary for survey administration. DRSS will procure and provide tablets preloaded with the digital survey application, printed paper surveys in all six languages, clipboards, pens, surveyor identification badges, high-visibility vests, printed outreach materials for posting on buses, and CTtransit All-Day Pass incentive cards. A reserve inventory of tablets and supplies will be maintained at a level sufficient to replace any equipment lost or damaged during the field period without disrupting data collection. All procurement costs are included in the project budget.

### Data Security Plan

All survey data will be stored on encrypted servers with access restricted to authorized project staff through role-based permissions. Paper surveys will be kept in locked storage at a secure facility and transported using chain-of-custody procedures. Digital survey responses will be transmitted using TLS 1.3 encryption and backed up daily to a secure off-site location. For the smartphone experience panel, GPS data will be stored with pseudonymous identifiers that have no linkage to personal information, for instance raw GPS traces will be kept separate from survey data and deleted at the conclusion of the project. DRSS will comply with all applicable state and federal data protection requirements and will describe the full data security framework in the Survey Management Plan for CTDOT's review and approval.

### Data Processing and Analysis

Paper surveys are digitized using an AI-powered OCR and handwriting recognition system that extracts responses and flags low-confidence items for human review, reducing manual data entry by an estimated 60 to 70 percent. All records enter a CTDOT-approved database within two weeks. Datasets are delivered in ASCII format with full documentation.

Digital survey responses are captured automatically in the database in real time. An AI-driven validation engine runs during completion, flagging suspicious patterns such as straight-lining across satisfaction items, impossibly fast completion times, and logically inconsistent answers. Flagged responses are routed to QA staff for human review rather than being automatically rejected, which preserves legitimate edge case responses while catching genuinely problematic data. During the processing phase, Dr. Greene will perform additional logic and consistency checks on all records, verifying that answers do not contradict one another and that response patterns fall within expected ranges. This real-time quality layer combined with post-collection verification means that data issues are identified and addressed at multiple stages rather than discovered only during final analysis.

Paper and digital data are combined using a unified coding scheme. Mode is treated as an analytic variable since the two modes may attract different respondent types. If significant mode effects are detected, propensity score weighting adjusts for differential selection, and reports present mode differences transparently.

Analyses target CTDOT's goal of identifying where satisfaction is low. They include descriptive statistics, frequency distributions, cross-tabulations by area, route, day type, and demographics, composite satisfaction indices, regression models for satisfaction drivers, factor analysis, and cluster analysis. Cell suppression thresholds protect against re-identification on low-ridership routes. Title VI data is analyzed per FTA guidance.

For open-ended responses, DRSS uses NLP to perform thematic coding and sentiment analysis, classifying responses into categories and assigning sentiment scores. Human coders validate a random sample to calibrate accuracy.

### Operational Data Integration

We also propose integrating CTtransit's AVL data into the survey analysis. At the Bidders Conference, CTDOT asked us to describe the need for and relevance of this approach, and subsequently confirmed that AVL data can be made available. Our rationale is straightforward in that the RFP asks riders to rate on-time performance, service frequency, and reliability. Without operational data for context, satisfaction scores alone cannot explain why riders feel the way they do. By matching AVL records to survey windows at the route-direction-time level, we can separate perception-driven from service-driven dissatisfaction and tell CTDOT exactly where to invest. The analysis adds minimal cost since the data already exists, and the aggregate-level linkage preserves anonymity.

### Smartphone Rider Experience Panel (Optional Enhancement)

As an optional analytical enhancement, DRSS proposes a supplementary smartphone-based rider experience panel of approximately 30 to 50 volunteer riders per service area in each year's wave (e.g., roughly 300 to 500 participants across the project). Panel members install a lightweight mobile app on their personal smartphones that passively captures GPS-based journey data (e.g., routes traveled, boarding and alighting locations, wait times, and transfer patterns) while prompting two to three brief experience questions at the conclusion of each trip. In exchange for participation, panel members receive a CTtransit All-Day Pass for each day they provide data, budgeted by DRSS.

The panel serves three purposes: it provides GPS-verified ground truth to validate self-reported survey data, it captures trip detail passively so the questionnaire does not have to burden respondents with itinerary questions, and for repeat riders it functions as a virtual diary capturing how perceptions change across trips over time.

Panel GPS data is stored with pseudonymous identifiers that have no linkage to personal information. Raw GPS traces are stored separately from survey data with restricted access and deleted at project completion. The panel is entirely opt-in, with informed consent obtained at enrollment.

### Reporting

DRSS will prepare ten individual Final Survey Reports, one for each CTtransit service area, and one Final Systemwide Survey Report. Each report includes an Executive Summary, a full analysis of survey results with written discussion and graphic and tabular illustrations, frequency distributions, cross-tabulations and correlations, an overview of the survey process and sampling methodology, a discussion of limitations, and overall data trends with meaningful correlations and recommendations for action. We will use a consistent analytical template across all ten reports while also including distinct analytical elements specific to each area's unique findings.

Reports incorporate AVL findings and NLP thematic summaries alongside standard satisfaction analysis. Each area report is submitted within one month of survey completion; the Systemwide Report by the end of Month 36. DRSS provides MS Word drafts for up to two rounds of CTDOT review, with finals as PDFs. Data file packages (raw and cleaned datasets in ASCII, codebooks, tabulations) accompany each report. Paper surveys are shipped to CTDOT within two months of the Systemwide Report.

## G. Suggested Modifications to the Scope of Services

DRSS offers two modifications for CTDOT's consideration. The first is the smartphone rider experience panel described in Section E. While the intercept survey addresses all RFP requirements on its own, the panel adds a longitudinal dimension, a ground truth validation mechanism, and a burden reduction strategy that would strengthen the research program. We have included the panel's costs in our budget, but if CTDOT prefers not to proceed with it, those funds can be reallocated to expand the intercept survey sample or support additional analytical work without exceeding the budget ceiling.

The second suggested modification is the development of a secure, web-based project dashboard that provides CTDOT staff with real-time visibility into survey progress by route, time period, and service area. The dashboard would supplement the weekly status reports with an interactive, always current view of data collection against targets, allowing CTDOT to monitor progress proactively rather than waiting for periodic written updates. The development cost would be modest and is not currently included in the budget, but could be incorporated if CTDOT is interested.

build_year <- function(yr) {
  pers_cost <- sum(personnel[[paste0("y", yr, "_cost")]])
