# Advice on grouping questions into a questionnaire

1. Early questions should be easy and pleasant to answer and should build rapport between the respondent and the interviewer, if administered in a telephone or face-to-face mode.
2. Questions at the beginning of a questionnaire should be engaging and explicitly address the topic of the survey, as it was described to the respondent prior to the interview.
3. Questions on the same topic should be grouped together.
4. Questions on the same topic should proceed from general to specific.
5. Questions on sensitive topics that might make respondents uncomfortable should be placed toward the end of the questionnaire.
6. Filter questions should be included to avoid asking respondents questions that do not apply to them.

# Methods involving human experts

* **Expert review**
  * Tends to be informal, qualitative, drawing on experts with survey methodology and subject matter expertise
* **Questionnaire Appraisal System (QAS)**
  * Tends to be structured, standardized, coding from a checklist, drawing on coders who do not need expertise in survey methodology and/or subject matters

# Methods involving computer experts

* **Question understanding AID (QUAID)**
  * A computer tool developed to review survey questions for comprehension issues (e.g., unfamiliar technical terms, complex syntax, etc)
* **Survey Quality Predictor (SQP)**
  * A computer tool developed to predict reliability, validity, and quality

LAB-BASED METHODS

NORC Research Science

# Lab-based methods used to ask a small number of participants to come to a lab. Now these methods are increasingly conducted virtually

## Focus groups

* Discussions of small groups of about 8=10 participants led by a moderator; qualitative information collected; effective in uncovering what people think about a topic and the language and terms they use

## Cognitive interviews

* 1:1 in-depth, semistructured interview; qualitative information collected; great diagnostic tool effective in identifying problems people had encountered during the survey response process (esp. comprehension issues)

## Eye-tracking

* Uses infrared cameras to track people’s eye movements as they read and answer survey questions; (a large amount of) quantitative data on fixation count, fixation duration, rereads, pupil dilation; effective at identifying specific parts of survey questions that are difficult for respondents to understand; can also uncover usability problems

# Field-based methods require data collection from (a larger number of) actual participants

## Web probing

* Additional cognitive probes included in a web instrument to collect participants’ qualitative feedback on (selected) target survey items

## Respondents and Interviewer Debriefing

* Debriefing questions asked of respondents to collect their (quantitative and qualitative) feedback on survey process and specific survey items

* Quantitative or qualitative feedback collected from interviewers about survey process, particular respondents’ overall experience and/or experience with specific items

## Randomized Experiment

* Participants are randomly assigned to one of (typically) two versions of questions measuring the same concept included in the data collection

FIELD-BASED METHODS (2)

\*NORC Research Science

20

## Field-based methods require data collection from (a larger number of) actual participants

### Response latency

* A type of paradata collected during the data collection process; uses timestamps to derive duration of answering the full questionnaire, a module, or a particular question

### Behavior coding

* Coders observe live interviewing or listen to recordings of interviews to code interviewer and respondent behaviors deviating from the paradigmatic survey interview process
* Behavior codes can then be aggregated at survey item level, recording level, respondent level, interviewer level, or survey level

### Reliability and Validity

* Quantitative methods using survey items included in the data collection to calculate reliability and validity. Some methods need more than one survey item and/or more than one round of data collection

### Pilot test

* Dressed rehearsal from beginning to end

# Use of ML to Automate Processing of Audio Recordings

* **Processing of audio recordings is labor and time intensive, and costly**
  * Typically at most 10% of recordings are processed

* **Use of ML to develop a pipeline to automatically process 100% of recordings** (Sun & Yan, 2023; Yan, Sun, & Battalahalli, 2024; 2025)

Audio Recording $\rightarrow$ [Diarization / Speech-to-text / Acoustic Features] $\rightarrow$ Analytics $\rightarrow$ [Interviewer Falsification / Interviewer Performance / Question Assessment]

-Diarization: who speaks at which turn
  -turn start and end, speaker
-Speech-to-text: transcription
  -distance scores to understand question reading behavior
  -*analysis of content*
-Acoustic features:
  -emotion
  -*tone*

*NORC Research Science

## Three options:

-Adopting
-Adapting
-Developing a new questionnaire

### **Adopting**

* **Direct translation of a source questionnaire to a target language without making wording or construct changes**
* **Pros: easy, inexpensive, source quex tested;**
* **Cons:**
  * Imperfect mapping
  * Language vagueness and ambiguity
  * Social context
  * Numeracy

<!-- page 41 -->

QUESTIONNAIRE DEVELOPMENT FOR 3MC SURVEYS (2)

*NORC Research Science

41

---

## Three options:

-Adopting
-Adapting
-Developing a new questionnaire

### **Adapting**

* **Allows for deliberate changes in source or target questionnaire to better reflect cultural nuances**
* **Types of adaptation**
  * Terminological (e.g., school systems)
  * Language driven (e.g., grammatical gender in languages)
  * Convention driven (e.g., left to right vs. right to left)
  * Culture driven (e.g., using an inanimate object with a missing part in one country whereas other countries used a human with a missing body part in an intelligence scale)

**Pro: Cheaper than developing a new questionnaire**

QUESTIONNAIRE DEVELOPMENT FOR 3MC SURVEYS (2)

\*NORC Research Science

---

Three options:

-Adopting
-Adapting
-Developing a new questionnaire

### Developing a new questionnaire

* **When the source question doesn't exist; allowing for tailoring the questionnaire to the cross-cultural context**
* **Types of design**
  * Sequential: source quex developed and tested first than adopted/adapted into other languages
  * Parellel: source quex developed by a multicultural group using a common language and culturally anchored; quex translated and tested in different cultures/languages before finalized
  * Simultaneous: Beginning with a common concept, questions developed and translated in each language.

**Con: very expensive than adopting and adapting**

# Translation by humans

### On-the-fly translation

* Lacks standardization

### Single translation

* Cost-effective, but does not account for personal language/cultural biases

### Back translation

* Used to be a common approach but no longer recommended

### Parallel translation

* Requires reconciliation of differences and process of doing so

### Split translation

* Cost effective way to minimize translator bias and save time, but requires a review meeting

### Committee approach

* Produces optimal translation but time-consuming and expensive

# Translation by Computer/AI

## Machine Translation

* e.g., Google translate
* Based on dictionary, learning
* Ignores context; cultural, history, political and environmental factors; individual interpretation and use

## AI-Assisted Translation

* AI as a translator vs. AI as a reviewer
* AI struggled with survey-specific requirements (e.g., handling culturally sensitive terms), deviated from human versions in translation of response scales, instructions, and culturally embedded terminology
* Prompts, models, training data, and language pairs all affect AI performance
* Probably has the same limitations as machine learning (ignoring context; cultural/history/political/environmental factors; individual interpretation and use)

# Questionnaire design is not a simple task

**Question writers must understand the psychology of survey response to anticipate what could go wrong and what can be done to fix issues**

**It is critical to test survey questions before fielding**

**AI can help with question writing, question evaluation, and translation. But humans must stay in the loop and must make the final decision**

**Questionnaire design is both a science and an art. Empirical evidence is needed to advance the science part and experience builds up the art part**
