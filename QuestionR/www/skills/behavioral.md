# Behavioral items

Version: 0.2

Task file for questions about experiences, events and behaviours: what, when,
where, how often, how long, how many, how much. An objective truth exists in
principle, and recall is the main problem.

## Reflexes

### can_they_know
- **When:** Before writing any behavioural item.
- **Say:** Ask whether respondents noticed and stored the information at the time. Frequent, mundane, similar events blend together and are not stored one by one; dates are rebuilt, not remembered. If the information was probably never stored, say so and suggest records, a diary or a narrower question.
- **Check:** Is the item asking for something the respondent is likely to have stored and can retrieve?
- **Source:** Yan, IASS/WSS Session 1 (retrieval problems; writing behavioural questions 1 to 3, 7); SURV632 (encoding limits, generic memories)

### reference_period
- **When:** Every behavioural item about a period of time.
- **Say:** State the reference period at the start of the question, with clear start and end points. Use the shortest period that still captures the behaviour: shorter for frequent or forgettable events, longer only for rare, memorable ones. Prefer a period that ends at the present.
- **Never:** Do not use "past year", "recently" or "usually" without saying what they mean. Do not mix two periods in one item.
- **Check:** Is there one stated reference period with unambiguous boundaries, short enough for the behaviour?
- **Source:** Yan, IASS/WSS Session 1 (writing behavioural questions 8); SURV632 (telescoping; forward telescoping grows with the length of the period)

### bound_the_period
- **When:** Events could be wrongly pulled into the reference period.
- **Say:** Anchor the start of the period to a landmark the respondent will remember (a public event, a holiday, a personal milestone) or, in a panel, to the previous interview. Note the landmark must be chosen with local knowledge.
- **Source:** Yan, IASS/WSS Session 1 (writing behavioural questions 4 and 9); SURV632 (bounding; lifetime periods as retrieval cues)

### retrieval_cues
- **When:** The behaviour is easy to forget or under-report.
- **Say:** Give cues about what happened and where, not dates. Allow time: a lead-in that says the question needs thought, or a slower pace in interviewer modes.
- **Source:** Yan, IASS/WSS Session 1 (writing behavioural questions 4 and 10); Burton and Blair 1991 as cited by Yan; SURV632 (what and where cue better than when)

### decompose_with_care
- **When:** A broad category covers different kinds of event.
- **Say:** Split it into separate questions for distinct sub-categories when the events are distinct and memorable. Say in the notes that splitting raises reported totals and can over-report for frequent, similar events.
- **Never:** Do not decompose routine, indistinct events into many small categories.
- **Check:** If the item is decomposed, are the sub-categories distinct, non-overlapping and memorable?
- **Source:** Yan, IASS/WSS Session 1 (writing behavioural questions 5); Belli et al. 2000; SURV632 (decomposition can add error)

### how_many_or_how_often
- **When:** Asking for a frequency.
- **Say:** For rare, distinct events over a short period ask "how many times": respondents can count. For frequent, regular behaviour ask for the usual rate in a stated unit (times per week). Say which you chose and why.
- **Check:** Does the wording match how respondents will arrive at the answer, counting or a rate?
- **Source:** SURV632 (enumeration and rate strategies; Burton and Blair)

### open_number
- **When:** The answer is a number or an amount.
- **Say:** Ask for the number in an open field with the unit stated, and give the allowed range in programming. If a closed frequency scale is needed, build its categories around the realistic range for this population and note that respondents read the scale as information about what is normal.
- **Never:** Do not use vague quantifiers (often, sometimes, rarely) as response options for frequency.
- **Check:** Is a count or amount collected as an open number with a stated unit, or else with categories free of vague quantifiers?
- **Source:** Yan, IASS/WSS Session 1 (writing behavioural questions 12 to 14); SURV632 (scale values as information; rounding)

### does_it_apply
- **When:** The behaviour is not universal.
- **Say:** Ask a filter question first (whether at all in the period), then the amount; put the filter in the item's parts. Make zero and not-applicable separate, explicit answers.
- **Check:** Can a respondent who did not do the behaviour, or to whom it does not apply, answer truthfully?
- **Source:** Yan, IASS/WSS Session 2 (filter questions); Sturgis, Roberts and Robinson 2026 (applicability failures)

### placement
- **When:** Programming notes for behavioural items.
- **Say:** Note that behavioural items should come before attitude items on the same topic where the flow allows.
- **Source:** Yan, IASS/WSS Session 1 (writing behavioural questions 11)

## Prohibitions

- Never ask the respondent to add up, average or work out a percentage across several behaviours in one item.
