# Materials the skills draw on

The skill files in `../skills/` were drafted from the materials below, from
graduate course notes on cognition and survey measurement, and from standard
texts on survey error and the response process. Each rule in a skill file
names its own sources on its `Source:` line.

Only openly licensed files are kept in this folder. The others are listed so
you can find them; they are not redistributed here.

## Webinar series: questionnaire design (IASS / Washington Statistical Society)

1. Yan, Ting. *Fundamentals of Questionnaire Design.* IASS/WSS Webinar,
   Session 1. NORC at the University of Chicago.
2. Yan, Ting. *Beyond Writing Survey Questions.* IASS/WSS Webinar, Session 2.
   NORC at the University of Chicago.
3. Haensch, Anna-Carolina. *Large Language Models in Questionnaire
   Development.* IASS/WSS Webinar, Session 3. LMU Munich.

## Studies of AI models writing and reviewing survey questions

4. Fuchs, Anna, Anna-Carolina Haensch, and Wiebke Weber. *AI for Survey
   Design: Generating and Evaluating Survey Questions with Large Language
   Models.* LMU Munich, 2026.
5. Sturgis, Patrick, Caroline Roberts, and Thomas Robinson. 2026. *LLMs for
   Survey Pretesting: How Well Can Large Language Models Identify Flaws in
   Survey Questions?* Working Paper 15. London School of Economics and
   Political Science; University of Lausanne.
6. Behzad, Tina, Wenbo Li, Reuben Kline, and Klaus Mueller. 2026. "Do LLMs
   Ask the Right Questions? Evaluating GPT-Generated Surveys as Instruments
   for Measuring Social Attitudes." European Language Resources Association
   (ELRA).
7. Martini, Peter J., Patrick Scagnelli, and Marisol E. Trujillo Medina. 2026.
   "From Prompt to Practice: Evaluating Unedited AI-Generated Psychometric
   Scales." *Survey Practice* 20. https://doi.org/10.29115/SP-2026-0002
   (open access, CC BY-NC-ND 4.0; the PDF is in this folder).
8. Wang, Yan, Ziyi Guo, and Christopher McCarty. 2026. "Can Large Language
   Models Revolutionize Survey Research? Experiments with Disaster
   Preparedness Responses." arXiv:2605.19229. https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.19229
9. Buskirk, Trent D. 2025. "They May Be Large, But Should They Be In Charge?
   The Pros and Precarity of Using Large Language Models in Survey Research."
   2025 FedCASIC Virtual Conference, April 23, 2025.
